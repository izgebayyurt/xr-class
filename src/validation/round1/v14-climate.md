## First impression (start screen only)
What is this about? Sea-level rise: how many buildings in a small model city would flood if the sea rose 4 m.
What would you do first? Click one of the three guesses. I'd go with "Few (<15%)", because 4 m doesn't sound like much.

## After playing
What is it about / what was it trying to teach? A few metres of sea-level rise floods far more than you'd expect when lots of the land is low. In this (invented) city, 4 m floods 23 of 48 buildings (48%), and you can drag the sea level from 0 to 8 m and watch buildings turn red as their ground goes under.

## Scores (1–5)
- clear goal: 4
- clear first action: 5
- could finish it: 4
- shows WHY (5 = I could inspect the reasoning myself; 1 = it just told/animated the answer): 3
- looks right on a laptop (everything needed is visible, readable, nothing overlapping/hidden): 2

## Problems
- The main question/result panel floats right in front of the city and hides the back rows of buildings. The model also pokes through the panel's last line, so "Click a block to see why" is partly covered.
- The "Start over" button overlaps the "Few (<15%)" guess button. The guess labels are bigger than their buttons, so the text overflows the edges.
- I couldn't see the water rise. Moving the sea from 1 m to 8 m mostly changes the buildings' colour (red/purple tint). There is no visible water surface climbing the blocks, so "flooded" looks like recolouring rather than water.
- The left info panel uses a huge title-size font for a long sentence ("Scale model with invented land heights (1 m of sea = 2.5 cm)"). It dominates the screen and is tilted, while the actual rule sits in smaller text below it.
- My guess is only shown through button colours (Few turned red, Many green). Nothing says "you guessed few, it's actually many".
- At +1 m the readout says 7 of 48 = "15%", which sits right on the Few/Some boundary (<15% vs 15–40%), so it reads as ambiguous.
- The "why" (each block's ground height) depends on clicking individual blocks, which have no labels. I couldn't operate that with this tool, so the only reasoning I could check was the count against the slider.
- The main panel keeps saying "At +4 m … 23 of 48" while the slider is at another value. Only the right-hand panel updates.

## Best moment
Sliding the sea from 1 m to 4 m and seeing the count jump from 7 to 23 flooded buildings ("the last metre alone flooded 6"), which made the non-linear effect hit home.
