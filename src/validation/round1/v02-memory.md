## First impression (start screen only)
What is this about? A short-term memory test: objects appear on a table, vanish, and you put them back in their spots. It probably shows how few things people can hold in mind at once. What would you do first? Click one of the four boards (0-2, 3-4, 5-6, 7-9) to guess how many of 9 objects I'd place correctly, as the panel asks.

## After playing
What is it about / what was it trying to teach? It's a spatial memory span task in three rounds that start at 3 objects and presumably increase. You watch a 4-second countdown, the objects vanish, then it names one object at a time ("Where was the white cube?") and you click its spot. My guess suggests it wants to compare my prediction with what I actually remember, i.e. limits of working memory. I couldn't get past the first placement, so this is my inference.

## Problems
- I couldn't play the core task. Answering means clicking one of 12 dark discs on the table, and the discs have no labels, so my tool couldn't click them. I never got past "Where was the blue cube? (1 of 3)", so I couldn't check the scoring, later rounds or the comparison with my guess. My "shows WHY" score is a guess for that reason.
- The "4-second" countdown ran far slower than real time on my laptop: about 5 seconds per tick, roughly 20 s in total. If that happens for students too, the memory demand is much easier than stated and the result is skewed.
- The table is small and far from the camera. The objects are tiny (a white cube is a few pixels, cones are thin slivers), and the 12 discs are hard to tell apart, especially in the back row. On a laptop it's easy to misclick a spot or misread an object.
- The start screen already shows 9 objects on the table before you guess, which looks like a preview of the test. It's unclear whether you're meant to start memorising then.
- Once rounds start, nothing on screen shows my earlier guess, so I can't tell whether it will come back.

## Best moment
The plain question "Where was the white cube?" after the table went blank. It made me realise I hadn't encoded the white cube at all.
