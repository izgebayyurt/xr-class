## First impression (start screen only)
What is this about? The Pythagorean theorem: a 3-4-5 right triangle with a square on each side (9, 16, 25), and the b² square already cut into coloured pieces.
What would you do first? Answer the guess (Less / Equal / More) about whether a² + b² compares to c². The labels already say 9, 16 and 25, so the answer is basically given away.

## After playing
What is it about / what was it trying to teach? Proving a² + b² = c² by hand: you're meant to slide the four pieces of the b² square and the a² square into the c² square (a Perigal-style dissection) and see that they fill it exactly.

## Scores (1–5)
- clear goal: 4
- clear first action: 5
- could finish it: 1
- shows WHY (5 = I could inspect the reasoning myself; 1 = it just told/animated the answer): 4
- looks right on a laptop (everything needed is visible, readable, nothing overlapping/hidden): 3

## Problems
- The whole activity after the guess is dragging pieces ("carry each coloured piece into the big blue square… 0 of 5 placed"). The pieces have no labels or buttons, so I couldn't place anything with click-only input and got stuck at 0 of 5. There's no click-to-place fallback or "show me" option.
- The guess is never acknowledged. Less and Equal both go straight to the same "Test it" text, with no colour change and no "you guessed X". The square labels (9 + 16 vs 25) also spoil the question before you answer.
- The area labels on the squares (a² = 3×3 = 9 etc.) are tiny, around 8 px on a laptop, and hard to read.
- "Start over" is tilted at a sharp angle, sits lower than the other buttons and crowds the "More" button.
- The instruction "Pieces slide; they never turn" is useful, but nothing on screen shows which pieces are draggable or where they should go (no outline or targets in the blue square).

## Best moment
Seeing the b² square already cut into four coloured pieces next to the empty c² square: it sets up a real "can these fit?" test, not just a stated formula.
