## First impression (start screen only)
What is this about? A scale model of the solar system where the Sun is a 22 cm ball and 1 m = 6.3 million km; it's about how far apart things really are.
What would you do first? Pick one of the three distance guesses (50 cm / 3 m / 25 m) for how far Earth is from the Sun. I can't see the Sun "on my left" though, so I'd be guessing blind.

## After playing
What is it about / what was it trying to teach? How empty the solar system is: at one consistent scale Earth is a 2 mm grain 23.7 m (108 Sun-widths) from a 22 cm Sun, and you fly out to each planet to feel the distances.

## Scores (1–5)
- clear goal: 4
- clear first action: 5
- could finish it: 3
- shows WHY (5 = I could inspect the reasoning myself; 1 = it just told/animated the answer): 3
- looks right on a laptop (everything needed is visible, readable, nothing overlapping/hidden): 2

## Problems
- After arriving at a planet, the "Magnified xN so you can see it" panel sits on top of the Start over button and covers the Uranus/Neptune buttons; clicking Neptune from Jupiter did nothing, so the outer planets can't be reached once you've flown somewhere.
- The row of planet buttons is crammed: labels are wider than the buttons and run into each other ("MercurVenusEarth Mars Beltupite Satururanuleptune"), and the planet info panel also overlaps that row.
- Flights are long (~20–25 s to Mars/Jupiter) and clicks on other planets during a flight are silently ignored, with no "please wait" feedback.
- The Sun "on your left" and the "speck inside the yellow ring" are never on screen in the default laptop view, so the core comparison (tiny grain vs. 22 cm Sun far away) is only stated in text, not seen. The teal moon rings at Jupiter/Earth weren't visible either, and they have no labels, so they can't be clicked here.
- During a flight the view barely seems to change (same star background, same UI); the only sign of movement is the km counter.
- The guess buttons stay on screen (coloured red/green) for the whole session and add clutter.
- A dotted blue line runs from the panel up into the sky with no explanation on the first screen.

## Best moment
The guess reveal: "Earth is 23.7 m from the Sun = 108 Sun-widths… Earth itself is a 2.0 mm grain". It turns the scale into something I could picture.
