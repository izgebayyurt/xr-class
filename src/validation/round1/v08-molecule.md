## First impression (start screen only)
What is this about? The shape of a water molecule: whether H-O-H is straight or bent, and at what angle.
What would you do first? Click one of the three angle guesses (Straight 180 / Bent 105 / Bent 90). No molecule is on screen yet, so it's a pure guess.

## After playing
What is it about / what was it trying to teach? Why water is bent: with only the two O-H bonds repelling, the model straightens out to about 180°. Turn on the two lone pairs, which push harder, and they squeeze the hydrogens to 104° (104.5° in real water). It's electron-pair repulsion (VSEPR) shown as a live model.

## Problems
- With lone pairs on, the purple lone-pair clouds rise up behind and partly under the instruction panel, so the very thing that explains the bend is half hidden.
- The live "H-O-H = … deg" readout is tiny (around 9 px), floats on its own between the molecule and the buttons, and isn't attached to the angle. It's the key number, but easy to miss.
- The molecule sits well left of centre and in the bent state is seen at an angle, so one H looks foreshortened and the "O" label floats off the atom. It's hard to judge the angle by eye.
- After the guess the panel says "Next: a model that shows why." There's no Next button; it auto-advances after a few seconds, which left me looking for something to click.
- Bonds-only never fully reaches 180° (it hovers at 168–176° and creeps up slowly), and "Toggle lone pairs" back off sends it on a long slow drift, so the comparison takes patience.
- The old guess buttons disappear after the reveal, so there's no reminder of what I guessed when the model reaches 104°.

## Best moment
Toggling lone pairs on and watching the readout fall from ~175° to 104°, with the text "104 degrees here, 104.5 in real water. That is why water is bent."
