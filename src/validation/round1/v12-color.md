## First impression (start screen only)
What is this about? Colour mixing, comparing mixing light with mixing paint: what colour you see where a red lamp and a green lamp overlap.
What would you do first? Click a guess. I'd probably pick "Brown", because that's what red and green paint make.

## After playing
What is it about / what was it trying to teach? Light mixes by adding (red + green light makes yellow, and all three make white), while paint mixes by subtracting (each pigment absorbs light, so red + green paint makes a dark brown). You can change the lamp and paint strengths with sliders and switch between the two walls.

## Problems
- The colour wall sits in front of the right edge of the explanation panel and cuts off the end of every line ("G 1.…", "brighter t…", "press S… paint", "absorbs … darker than eith…"). That hides the key sentence.
- In paint mode the wall title "PAINT on white paper (a model)" is white text on a cream background. It is nearly unreadable and spills past the edge of the wall.
- My guess is never marked right or wrong. It just says "You guessed Brown" and then shows the answer, so a newcomer has to work out from the text that the guess was wrong.
- The colour names are coarse. Red 0.5 + green 1.0 light clearly shows yellow-green on the wall, but the panel calls it "green".
- The "why" for paint is a single sentence ("each pigment keeps a share of the light"). There are no numbers like the R/G/B readout in light mode, so the paint result is asserted rather than something I can check.
- "Click a spot / click where two circles overlap" is the main way to explore, but the spots have no labels. Clicking the wall title did nothing, so I couldn't test that part with this tool.
- Each slider shows its value twice ("Red light 0.50" and "0.5"), which is clutter. The "B" label shows with no blue circle when blue is at 0, which is confusing at first.

## Best moment
Turning the blue lamp up and seeing the familiar RGB diagram appear, with a white centre plus cyan and magenta overlaps, then pressing Show paint and watching the same three circles go dark.
