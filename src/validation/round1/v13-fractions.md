## First impression (start screen only)
What is this about? Comparing fractions using two same-size pizzas: 1/8 against 1/4.
What would you do first? Click the pizza that has more on it, which is the 1/4 one.

## After playing
What is it about / what was it trying to teach? Comparing fractions by cutting both pizzas into same-size slices, for example 1/4 = 2/8 against 1/8, or 5/6 = 10/12 against 3/4 = 9/12, and then counting. It also tackles the idea that a bigger bottom number means a bigger fraction. There are 5 rounds, then a score.

## Scores (1–5)
- clear goal: 5
- clear first action: 5
- could finish it: 5
- shows WHY (5 = I could inspect the reasoning myself; 1 = it just told/animated the answer): 4
- looks right on a laptop (everything needed is visible, readable, nothing overlapping/hidden): 3

## Problems
- The pizzas sit at the very bottom of the screen. Their front edges are cut off by the window, and the "WEBXR NEEDS HTTPS" button covers the middle of the table between them. The slices you are supposed to count are the lowest and least readable thing on screen.
- The "Start over" label is much bigger than its small brown block, so the words hang off both edges and look broken. It also sits out on its own to the right of everything else.
- The re-cut pizzas in the 12ths round are crowded and seen at a steep angle. Counting 10 against 9 slices by eye is hard. I relied on the "= 10/12" labels instead.
- Small grammar slip in the feedback: "1 slices vs 2 slices".
- The final message says "Good try!" for every score, including 0 out of 5.
- (Tool limit) I could click a pizza but couldn't choose which one, since only the pair responds as a single object, so my answers were effectively random. The feedback for a wrong answer was still clear.

## Best moment
After I answered, the 1/4 pizza re-cut itself into eighths and its label changed to "1/4 = 2/8", so I could see the two slices for myself.
