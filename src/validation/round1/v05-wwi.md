## First impression (start screen only)
What is this about? How narrow a WWI trench was, i.e. how many soldiers could stand shoulder to shoulder across its floor. You're standing at the mouth of a dark trench. What would you do first? Click one of the guess boards 1, 2 or 3. The panel already gives "floor 0.9 m, shoulders 0.5 m", so it reads like an arithmetic question with the answer handed over.

## After playing
What is it about / what was it trying to teach? How cramped trenches were: one man's shoulders fill about 56% of the floor width, so only one soldier fits across and passing someone means squeezing by. After the guess it shows a row of soldier blocks (green fits, red "inside the wall"), then tells you to walk forward into the trench and read signs.

## Problems
- The question gives the answer away. The panel states 0.9 m and 0.5 m before you guess, so the guess is just 0.9 ÷ 0.5 rounded down, with nothing to predict about the trench itself.
- The demonstration contradicts itself. The text says the second man ends up "inside the wall (red, left)", but the red blocks are to the right of the green one. The three blocks sit together on a frame floating off to the left of the trench, not on the trench floor, so it looks like all three fit. It also always shows 3 men (1 green, 2 red), whether I guessed 2 or 3.
- The rest of the content is out of reach. The panel says "Walk forward into the trench and read the signs", but no signs are visible from the start position. Walking needs WASD, which my tool couldn't do, so I never saw them. There's no Start over and the guess boards stop responding after the first answer, so you can't retry.
- It's very dark. The trench interior and its far end are near-black brown on brown, and on a laptop screen you can't make out anything inside.
- The panel sits right in the trench opening and covers the view down the trench you're asked to walk into.
- There's no sense of scale. Nothing (a person, a ruler, the floor marked 0.9 m) lets you see the width yourself, so you have to take "56%" on trust.
- A yellow "Scene check: 0 errors · 1 warning — show report" developer badge is visible.

## Best moment
The line "one man's shoulders fill 56% of the floor width, so passing means squeezing by" turns a dry number into something you can picture.
