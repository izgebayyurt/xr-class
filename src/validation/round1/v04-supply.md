## First impression (start screen only)
What is this about? Pricing a lemonade stand: how raising the price changes profit when each shopper has a maximum they'll pay. 12 blue shoppers are lined up on both sides. What would you do first? Click one of the three guesses below the panel ("Always up", "Up, then down", "Always down").

## After playing
What is it about / what was it trying to teach? Profit = (price − cost) × number of buyers, so raising the price helps until losing shoppers costs more than the extra margin. Profit rises, peaks ($14 at $2.50 with 7 buyers), then falls to $0 once nobody buys. A price slider updates which shoppers buy (green or grey), a live readout, and a graph of profit and buyers against price.

## Problems
- The graph shows the whole answer before you touch the slider: the full profit curve with its peak and the full buyers line. That spoils "find your best price", since you can just read the peak off the graph.
- The verdict comes too early and is slightly misleading. The explanation locks in at the first profit drop ($2.50 → $2.75: "profit fell… 'Up, then down': correct"), but profit rises again at $3.25 ($13.75) before falling. The panel never mentions this, and a student who keeps sliding may think "Up, then down" is wrong.
- The graph is hard to read. The y-axis runs 0–15 but is shared by profit ($) and shoppers (count) with no second scale. The moving dot sits on the shoppers line, not the profit line, so it doesn't show where your profit is. The caption bar above the graph is wider than the graph and slants off its left edge.
- Panels cover the scene. The main panel hides the "LEMONADE" sign and the stand. The price readout panel sits in front of the right-hand shoppers, so their green/grey state is partly hidden. The sun overlaps the help box in the top-left.
- "Point at a shopper to see their limit" only works on hover. Shoppers have no visible labels, so I couldn't check any individual limit, and the core data behind the curve stays hidden unless you know to hover.
- The slider snaps to $0.25 steps (2.4 → $2.50, 2.9 → $3.00) without saying so, and the "Price per cup" label floats well above the slider.
- A yellow "Scene check: 0 errors · 28 warnings — show report" developer badge is visible to students.

## Best moment
Moving from $2.50 to $3.00 and getting a concrete explanation: "+$0.50 per cup, but 2 fewer shoppers (7 to 5), so profit fell from $14.00 to $12.50", while two shoppers turned grey.
