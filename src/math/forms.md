# Google Forms for the PME study

Two forms. Type the questions exactly as written: the pipeline matches answers to questions by these titles (the bold part).

---

## Form 1 — "Design a math experience" (in class)

**Description:** Imagine a friend who finds math hard. Design a short interactive experience that teaches them one math idea. An AI will build it from your answers, and you'll try it later. There are no wrong answers.

| # | Question title | Type | Required |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Your name** (first and last) | Short answer | yes |
| 2 | **Which idea do you want to teach?** | Multiple choice (the 12 options below) | yes |
| 3 | **If you chose "Something else", what idea?** | Short answer | no |
| 4 | **What do you already know about this idea?** Explain it in your own words. It's fine to be unsure. | Paragraph | yes |
| 5 | **What would your friend get wrong at first, and why?** | Paragraph | no |
| 6 | **How would you show your friend why the right answer is right?** What would they see or do? | Paragraph | yes |
| 7 | **Walk through it: what happens first, next, and at the end?** | Paragraph | no |
| 8 | **Look and feel** (colors, style, a setting or a story) | Short answer | no |
| 9 | **Flat or 3D?** | Multiple choice: Flat (2D) / 3D / No preference | no |
| 10 | **What do you expect the finished experience to be like?** Describe what you picture. | Paragraph | yes |
| 11 | **How well do you expect it to help your friend understand?** | Linear scale 1–5 (1 = Not at all, 5 = Very well) | yes |

Options for question 2 (students see only these questions, not the answers):
1. Is 0.999… (the 9s go on forever) equal to 1, or just very close to 1?
2. Which is bigger: 5/9 or 4/7?
3. A price goes up 25% on Monday and down 25% on Tuesday. Is it back where it started?
4. You invest $1,000 at 7% a year. Roughly how much do you have after 30 years?
5. A box is made twice as long, twice as wide and twice as tall. How much more paint covers it, and how much more water does it hold?
6. "There are six times as many students as professors." How do you write that as an equation with S and P?
7. Monty Hall: you pick one of three doors, and the host opens another door showing a goat. Should you switch?
8. Rolling two dice: is a 5 and a 6 more likely than two 6s, or equally likely?
9. Class A (10 students) averages 70 on a test; class B (30 students) averages 90. What is the average of all 40 students?
10. A big hospital and a small hospital: which has more days when over 60% of the babies born are boys?
11. Counting one number every second, how long would it take to reach a million? A billion?
12. Something else (describe below)

Settings: no sign-in needed. If a student submits twice, the later answer (same name) replaces the earlier one. Names stay in a private roster; everything else uses a participant ID (p01, p02, …), and the builder never sees names.

## Form 2 — "Scene feedback" (after the scenes are built)

The AI built every design twice, as **version A** and **version B**. One version comes from Opus 5.5 and the other from Sonnet 5.5 (the free model). Which model is A is randomized and balanced, and students are never told which is which. Students find their name in the shared sheet. Their row has six "Open" links (both versions of their own design and both versions of two classmates' designs, with no names) and a "Give feedback" link. That link opens this form with their participant ID and all six scene codes already filled in. Each scene shows its code in the top-right corner.

Use the same scales in every section so the answers can be compared.

**Section 1 — Your design.** "Open both versions of your own design and try each one the way your friend would."
| # | Question title | Type | Required |

| 1 | **Participant ID (filled in for you)** | Short answer | yes |
| 2 | **Version A code** | Short answer | yes |
| 3 | **Version B code** | Short answer | yes |

| 5 | **Version B: how closely did it match what you expected?** | Linear scale 1–5 (Not at all → Exactly) | yes |
| 6 | **Version A: how much does it feel like your own design?** | Linear scale 1–5 (Not at all → Completely) | yes |
| 7 | **Version B: how much does it feel like your own design?** | Linear scale 1–5 (Not at all → Completely) | yes |
| 8 | **Version A: is the math correct?** | Multiple choice: Yes / I think there's a mistake / Not sure | yes |
| 9 | **Version B: is the math correct?** | Multiple choice: Yes / I think there's a mistake / Not sure | yes |

Grid option: Google Forms "Multiple choice grid" (rows: Version A, Version B; columns: 1–5) is shorter on screen. But the pre-filled link can only fill text fields, so keep the six code fields as Short answer.

### After you build Form 2
Open ⋮ → **Get pre-filled link**, then type these into the code fields:
- `PID` into Participant ID
- `OA` and `OB` into Version A code and Version B code
- `S1A` and `S1B` into the two Design 1 codes
- `S2A` and `S2B` into the two Design 2 codes

Then click **Get link** → **Copy link**, and give that link to the run-day chat.
